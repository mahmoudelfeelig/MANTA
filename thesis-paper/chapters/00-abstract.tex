\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

This thesis investigates MANTA (Mobile Anomaly and Network Threat Analysis), an anomaly-first
hybrid intrusion-detection system for encrypted mobile and endpoint traffic that operates
on flow metadata rather than packet payloads. The implemented artifact spans three modules: 
an Android endpoint based on \texttt{VpnService} \citep{android_vpnservice}, a backend adapter
for authenticated ingest and analyst feedback, and a reproducible machine-learning pipeline
for training, privacy benchmarking, and model export.

The evaluation uses a public-only merged corpus of 6,034,487 flows drawn from seven dataset
sources and 904,371 aggregated 60-second windows. It compares on-device linear and
TFLite-compatible detectors, remote hybrid models, privacy-student variants, federated simulation,
and privacy-reduced feature views under grouped, source-aware splits. The strongest anomaly result
in the March 2026 matrix is a remote gradient-boosted tree with F1 \(0.449\), PR-AUC \(0.482\), and
ROC-AUC \(0.765\), while lighter on-device models remain substantially weaker.

The privacy evaluation separates two attack surfaces. \emph{Release leakage} is inference from the
feature representation that the system intentionally exports or shares. \emph{Observer leakage} is
inference from the encrypted traffic sequence visible to an on-path network observer before any
export-time reduction is applied. The reduced privacy views (\texttt{medium} and \texttt{strict})
preserve anomaly utility in the March 2026 ablation study and lower exact app-identification leakage
from \(0.235\) normalized leakage in the full view to \(0.113\) and \(0.099\), respectively.
However, stronger metadata leakage remains high for app family, destination behavior, and dataset
source, and the encrypted-flow sequence audit shows severe observer leakage, with normalized
leakage \(0.544\) for app identification and \(0.981\) for dataset-source inference. I conclude
that feature-level coarsening and privacy-aware representation learning improve the released
representation, but do not hide the traffic patterns available to passive observers.
